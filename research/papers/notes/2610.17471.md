## Setup
- Tasks / benchmarks: ProgramBench (200 long-horizon repo-reconstruction tasks from black-box binaries, e.g., CMatrix, Ripgrep, FFmpeg, PHP Interpreter; Section 3, p. 5) and Commit0-Lite (16 Python repo reimplementation tasks; Section 3 & Appendix C.13).
- Models used: GPT-5.6 family: Sol (strong/frontier), Terra (mid-tier), Luna (cheap/small), all with medium reasoning effort (Section 3, p. 5). Also Claude and Gemini evaluations in Appendix C.
- Baselines: Solo agent (single-agent ReAct bash loop with minimal and heavy guidance) vs homogeneous teams vs heterogeneous teams. Budget matching: evaluated across empirical Pareto cost curves (Eq. 1-2, Section 2, p. 4).

## Topology tested
- Depth: 1 (Hierarchical lead-worker topology: lead agent receives task, decomposes it, spawns workers via `delegate_agent(task, agent_id)`, collects terminal outputs, and synthesizes; Section 3, p. 4; Figure 1, p. 3).
- Width: Dynamic team size chosen by lead (averaging 4.2–5 workers; Section 4, p. 5) and fixed team sizes varied systematically at 2, 4, 8, and 16 workers (Section 7, p. 9–10).
- Roles: Lead agent (coordinator/integrator/synthesizer; also handles non-delegated stages), Worker agents (assigned to Planning [P], Implementation [I], Verification [V], or full workflow [PIV]).
- Communication / handoff format: Boundary-only handoff (lead passes subtask prompt; worker terminal output returns on completion). Direct peer-to-peer inter-agent messaging tested in Section 8 via `send_message` and `list_agents`. Implementers operate in shared sandbox but guided to edit disjoint modules (Section 3, p. 4; Appendix D).

## Findings (quote numbers with table/figure/page refs)
- Depth: Depth-1 lead-worker hierarchy outperforms solo agent across both minimal and heavy PIV guidance, but homogeneous teams cost 3× to 7× as much in inference dollars (Section 4, p. 5; Figure 2).
- Width: Planning workers scale monotonically up to 16 workers (2 workers: +3.2% reward over solo; 16 workers: +7.1% reward over solo; Section 7, p. 9; Figure 8). In sharp contrast, implementation and verification workers plateau or degrade beyond 4 workers (from 4 to 16 workers, reward drops by -0.015 points for implementation and -0.011 points for verification; Section 7, p. 9).
- Role → model placement:
  * Strong lead is mandatory: Lead model sets the ceiling of team performance; upgrading lead with identical Luna workers raises reward by +20.9% (Luna→Terra) and +15.4% (Terra→Sol). A Terra lead + Luna workers trails a solo Sol agent by 10.3% (Section 6, p. 9; Figure 7).
  * Cheap models pay off as planners, but ruinous as implementers: Pairing a Sol lead with cheap Luna planners recovers 47.5% of the all-Sol team's reward gain for only 13.9% of the additional cost ($\eta_P = 51.0$ vs $\eta_{Sol} = 3.8$, a ~13× increase in reward per dollar; Section 6, p. 8–9).
  * Cheap implementers cause severe degradation: Luna implementers reduce team reward below that of the solo Sol agent with negative marginal efficiency ($\eta_I = -12.0$; Section 6, p. 8–9; Figure 6).
  * Overall Pareto configuration: A heterogeneous team of 1 Sol lead + 16 Luna planners comes within 1.4 points of an all-Sol team while cutting total inference cost by 70% (Section 1, p. 2; Section 7, p. 9; Figure 8).
  * Planning delegation dominance: Delegating only planning (P) to workers while the lead implements and verifies matches the reward of delegating all three phases (PIV) at 50% lower cost ($\eta_P = 3.8$ vs $\eta_{PIV} = 1.4$; Section 5, p. 6–7; Figure 4).
- Verification / review effects: Delegating verification to workers yields low marginal return ($\eta_V = 1.7$ vs $\eta_P = 3.8$; Figure 4, p. 7). Luna verifiers leave reward unchanged relative to lead-only verification (Section 6, p. 8). Verifier reports provide diminishing returns beyond 4 workers because they repeat known test failure modes without discovering new code state (Section 7, p. 9–10).
- Cost / tokens / latency: Homogeneous Sol blocking teams take 3.5×–3.8× longer wall-clock time than solo agents (Section 4, p. 6). Asynchronous teams save latency (1.4×–3.1× solo time), but under minimal guidance lose ~6 reward points because the lead acts prematurely on partial results before workers settle; heavy guidance instructing lead to wait for all reports recovers full reward (Section 4, p. 6).
- Failure modes observed: (1) Asynchronous leads abandoning or acting prematurely on partial worker outputs unless explicitly constrained to wait; (2) Cheap worker implementers generating broken code that overwhelms the lead's integration capacity; (3) Direct inter-agent communication channels causing performance drops of 4.4–7.1 points in weaker models due to chatter and distraction, with 84% of messages flowing back to lead anyway (Section 8, p. 10; Figure 9).

## Implications for Hannibal's Army (label each as INFERENCE)
- Depth-1 design: INFERENCE: A strict Depth-1 topology (General/Orchestrator → Soldiers/Workers) is highly effective provided the Orchestrator enforces barrier synchronization (must await all worker reports before synthesizing, matching Samway's finding where unconstrained async leads lost 6 points).
- Depth-2 design: INFERENCE: In a two-level command hierarchy (Field Marshal → Generals → Soldiers), sub-orchestrators (Generals) must be frontier-class reasoners because lead capability strictly bounds performance ceiling (Terra leads trailed solo Sol by 10.3%); cheap models must never serve as Generals.
- Which agent/model type goes in which seat:
  * Field Marshal / Root Coordinator: Strong frontier tier (GPT-6.1-Sol / Opus) to set architectural ceiling and global task bounds.
  * Sub-orchestrators / Generals: Strong frontier tier (Sonnet / Sol) for decomposition, integration, and barrier gating.
  * Explorers / Planners: Cheap/fast tier (Gemini Flash / Luna) scaled widely (up to 8–16 workers) to gather repo intelligence, map interfaces, and discover failure modes.
  * Implementers / Coders: High-capability tier ONLY (Sonnet / Terra / Sol); cheap models in implementation seats create negative return ($\eta = -12.0$) and break integration.
  * Verifiers / Reviewers: Moderate to strong tier; cap verifier fan-out at $\le 4$ workers because verifiers plateau rapidly.

## Validity
- Evidence grade: B (Preprint [Oct 2026], highly controlled ablation on 200 benchmark tasks with 3 runs, coordinate-wise Pareto search, bootstrap 95% intervals).
- Threats: ProgramBench focuses on black-box repository reconstruction (which naturally privileges early exploratory planning over implementation); findings on Commit0 showed implementation benefits more when skeleton/plan is already provided (Appendix C.13).
